BrewberryPi / Privacy

Privacy policy

What the BrewberryPi website, the recipe editor and the software do with your data: as little as possible.

Last changed 6 November 2026

In short

BrewberryPi is a free, open-source hobby project. It runs no servers of its own and keeps no user accounts. This page covers the website brewberrypi.org, the recipe editor recipes.brewberrypi.org and the BrewberryPi software on your Raspberry Pi.

The website brewberrypi.org

The pages are served by GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.). GitHub processes technical request data, such as your IP address, to deliver them; see the GitHub privacy statement.

Page views are counted with GoatCounter, a privacy-friendly web analytics service. It uses no cookies and does not store IP addresses or anything that identifies you. It records the page, the referring page, the browser and operating system, the screen size and the country (derived from the IP address, which is then discarded). The counts are only used to see which pages are read. See GoatCounter's privacy policy. Content blockers can turn the counting off; the site works the same.

The recipe editor recipes.brewberrypi.org

The recipe editor runs entirely in your browser. There is no BrewberryPi server behind it: your recipes and your Google account data never pass through or are stored by BrewberryPi. It counts no page views.

Without signing in

Recipes you create or open are stored only in your browser's own storage (IndexedDB) on your device, so they are still there after a reload. They are not sent anywhere. Files you download are saved by your browser on your device. Clearing your browser data deletes the stored recipes.

Signed in with Google

When you sign in with Google, the editor asks Google for access to only the files it creates or that you open with it in your Google Drive (Google's drive.file permission). It cannot see any other file in your Drive. With this access, and only to show and save your recipes, your browser talks directly to Google:

  • it creates the folder BrewberryPi with the subfolder recipes in your Drive;
  • it lists, reads, writes, renames and moves to the trash the recipe files in that folder;
  • it reads your name and e-mail address to show which account is signed in.

The access token Google issues expires after about an hour. Until then, or until you sign out, it is kept in your browser's local storage on this device, so other tabs of the editor and reloads stay signed in; signing out in one tab signs out all of them and removes it. To make the next sign-in quicker, the editor also remembers your e-mail address there until you sign out. Nothing else is stored. No data from Google is shared with anyone, sold, used for advertising, or used to train AI models.

The recipe editor's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

You can remove the editor's access at any time in your Google account under Third-party apps & services. Your recipe files stay in your Drive until you delete them.

The editor is served by GitHub Pages as well (see above). Google's sign-in and file picker are loaded from Google when you use them; Google's own privacy policy applies to them.

The BrewberryPi software on your Raspberry Pi

The BrewberryPi app and BrewberryPi OS run on your own Raspberry Pi in your own network. They send no data to BrewberryPi. The device contacts the internet only for what you start yourself, such as downloading updates from GitHub.

Your rights and contact

BrewberryPi keeps no personal data about you, so there is nothing to look up, correct or delete on our side. For data held by GitHub, GoatCounter or Google, use their privacy settings and contacts linked above.

Questions about this policy: open an issue at github.com/tinkerius/brewberrypi.
