BrewberryPiThe DIY Homebrewing Controller for Raspberry Pi

A Raspberry Pi controller for an electric mash kettle: software that runs your recipe, and a 3D-printable case that holds the electronics.

Get started

From parts to first brew
in three steps.

  1. 1

    Print, assemble and wire

    Print the case, assemble the Pi, the relay board and the power parts in it, and wire the probe and the buzzer. The wiring guide shows every pin.

    The case → Wiring guide →

  2. 2

    Flash the image

    Write BrewberryPi OS to an SD card with Raspberry Pi Imager; the app is included. Set a host name and your Wi-Fi, or set Wi-Fi up later through its own hotspot.

    Flashing guide →

  3. brewberrypi.local 3

    Open it and brew

    Open http://brewberrypi.local/ on your phone (use the host name you chose), load a recipe and press start.

    User manual →

BrewberryPi switches a mains-powered heater. A printed case is not mains insulation on its own. Have the mains wiring done or checked by a qualified electrician.

The software

Flash it.
Brew with it.

The brewing app and its operating system as one image for the Raspberry Pi. You use it from the browser on your phone.

Runs your recipe

Heats, holds each rest to the minute and switches the pump, step by step.

Live in any browser

Phone, tablet or PC, several at once, all showing the same brew.

Asks when it's your turn

Water in, malt in, iodine test: it asks on screen and a buzzer reminds you.

One image for Pi 3, 4, 5

Flash it with Raspberry Pi Imager. Updates come from the browser, with rollback.

The case

Print the box.
Plug in the kettle.

A 3D-printable enclosure for the Pi, the relay board and the power parts, made to stand next to the kettle.

4 printed parts

Body, divider and two lids, on any FDM printer; each fits within 200 mm.

Everything inside

The Pi, the relay board and the power supply; a divider keeps mains apart.

Switches and sockets

Main power, heater and pump switches at the front; sockets at the back.

Made from a spec

Designed in OpenSCAD from a written specification, every dimension a parameter.
