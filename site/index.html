Mash by recipe.
Not by guesswork.

A Raspberry Pi controller for an electric mash kettle: software that runs your recipe, and a 3D-printable case that holds the electronics.

Get started → See the parts

The project

Two parts.
One brewing controller.

The software runs on the Raspberry Pi and you use it from your phone. The case holds the Pi, the relays and the power parts next to the kettle. Use both, or just the one you need.

Part 1

The software

  • Runs your recipe step by step: heats, holds each rest, switches the pump
  • Live in any browser: phone, tablet or PC
  • Asks you when it's your turn: water, malt, iodine test, with a buzzer
  • One image for Raspberry Pi 3, 4 and 5, with updates and rollback

Part 2

The case

  • 3D-printable on any FDM printer, 4 parts
  • Holds the Pi, the relay board and the power supply; a divider keeps mains apart
  • Switches for main power, heater and pump; sockets for heater and pump
  • Designed from a written spec in OpenSCAD

Get started

From parts to first brew
in three steps.

  1. 1

    Print and wire

    Print the case and fit the Pi, the relay board, the DS18B20 probe and the buzzer. The wiring guide shows every pin.

    The case → Wiring guide →

  2. 2

    Flash the image

    Write BrewberryPi OS to an SD card with Raspberry Pi Imager; the app is included. Set a host name and your Wi-Fi, or set Wi-Fi up later through its own hotspot.

    Flashing guide →

  3. 3

    Open it and brew

    Open http://brewberrypi.local/ on your phone (use the host name you chose), load a recipe and press start.

    User manual →

BrewberryPi switches a mains-powered heater. A printed case is not mains insulation on its own. Have the mains wiring done or checked by a qualified electrician.
